# PRD Quality Review — chordslanka.lk

## Overall verdict

**Strong draft, not yet green-light.** This is a genuinely good PRD: it has a thesis it bets on ("Every other chord site in this market is a catalogue you read. This one is an instrument you play with.", §1), the features serve that thesis rather than a wishlist, the 32 FRs carry real testable consequences instead of acceptance-criteria theatre, and §5/§6.2/§18 make omissions explicit rather than leaving them to inference. What holds up best is the player-facing core — §4.2 Play View, §4.3 Transpose/Capo, §4.8 Advertising — where requirements are bounded, mutually consistent, and honest about the trade-offs they cost.

What is at risk is the seam between the document's own risk analysis and its requirements. Three capabilities that §6.1 and §13.1 commit to for MVP — the chord-only/lyrics-removed fallback, the takedown request mechanism and log, and analytics instrumentation for SM-1..SM-8 — have no FR at all, which means the mitigations for the PRD's #1 existential risk cannot be broken into stories. Alongside that, §6.1 commits the Ingestion scope that OQ-3 calls "Unresolved and gating" and the full-lyric catalogue that OQ-4 says "Blocks advertising approval", with no decision gate, no fallback branch, and no phasing across 10 feature areas for a single operator. The PRD is ready for the founder to review and decide; it is not ready to hand to epic breakdown as-is.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions, not smuggled in as considerations. §4.3 says "Sheets always open in their Original Key — the original is the starting truth, not a preference", which is a choice with a named loser (returning-visitor convenience) rather than a balance. §12 "AdSense only" closes off subscriptions, donations, affiliate and sponsored content in one line. §4.8 refuses to smooth its central tension — "AdSense is the entire business model, and the founder named uncontrolled advertising as the worst possible version of this product. Both things are true" — and then resolves it with an absolute rule rather than a hedge. §4.9 takes the offline rejection and immediately books its cost in the §4.9 `[ASSUMPTION]`: "a Sheet already on screen may not survive a total loss of connectivity mid-song, which is a real risk at gatherings in poor coverage". A reader pushing back on any of those would find their objection already on the page.

The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at real tensions rather than safe checkpoints. The §4.3 note is the bravest thing in the document: it admits that "The brainstorm identified a capability that no requirement above delivers: helping a player work out *which way to transpose*", names it "the root cause the whole product traces back to", and declines to specify it. That is the opposite of a PRD smoothing itself to neutral. The §4.8 note refuses to convert secondary-source ad rate figures into a projection ("This is market context gathered from secondary sources, not a projection for this site"), and the §6.2 note flags that removing the founder's own original Learn/Practice/Mastered sketch "should be confirmed deliberately".

Where decision-readiness falls short is sequencing. §17 contains eleven Open Questions, two of which are self-declared blockers, yet §6.1 commits their dependent scope unconditionally — "Catalogue Ingestion with human approval against the Source Artefact (FR-24 to FR-26)" and the full lyric catalogue — as though both had resolved favourably. §14 lists mitigations for "Ingestion accuracy too low to be usable" and "Advertising account refused or suspended over reproduced lyrics", but nothing in the document says what scope survives if the OQ-3 spike returns unusable accuracy or OQ-4's counsel says do not publish lyrics in full. A decision-maker can act on the individual choices here; they cannot act on the question "can we start building, and on what."

### Findings

- **high** No decision gate between the gating Open Questions and committed MVP scope (§6.1, §17 OQ-3/OQ-4, §14) — OQ-3 is labelled "Unresolved and gating" and OQ-4 "Blocks advertising approval and shapes the data model", but §6.1 lists their dependent scope as committed with no pass/fail gate, no fallback branch, and no statement of what the release becomes if either resolves badly. Downstream epic breakdown will read §6.1 as buildable. *Fix:* add a short "Release gates" subsection to §6 stating which OQs must close before build starts versus before public launch, and for OQ-3 and OQ-4 name the fallback scope explicitly (chord-only catalogue; manual-transcription-only growth rate).
- **medium** Open Questions carry no owner, no by-when, and no blocks-what tag (§17) — only OQ-1 is ranked ("the highest-value unanswered product question") and only OQ-3/OQ-4 hint at what they block. The other eight sit at equal apparent weight, so the founder reviewing this has no triage order. *Fix:* tag each OQ with owner, target date, and what it blocks (build / launch / v2).

## Substance over theater — strong

The content is earned. §1 Vision could not be dropped into another PRD in this category: it names a specific moment ("someone at a gathering asks for a song, and the guitarist has seconds to find it"), a specific market condition ("the incumbent added roughly fifteen songs in six months while advertising daily updates, its newest album collection is from January 2024"), and derives the business constraint from the product rather than bolting it on ("An interrupted chorus costs a returning visitor, and returning visitors are the only asset the business has").

There is no persona theater. Five UJs, five named protagonists, and each one drives at least one requirement that would otherwise not exist: Nuwan's Google-entry path (§2.3 UJ-1, "arriving from a Google search for the song name — not from the homepage") is why FR-2 demands the first Sheet section in the initial server response; Ishara's inability to find a key by ear is why FR-11 and FR-13 exist and why OQ-1 is open at all; Priyan's "screenshot habit" produces FR-18/FR-19 and the §4.5 framing "Favourites and Collections exist to beat the screenshot"; Sanduni produces FR-21's structured-proposal shape; Lahtlk (UJ-5) produces the whole of §4.7. Remove any protagonist and requirements disappear with them.

Most notably, §16 actively dismantles its own would-be innovation section. "The strategic conclusion: catalogue size and transpose are table stakes in this market, not differentiators" is a PRD telling itself that two of its own features are not the bet — including transpose, which §4.3 spends four FRs on. The slchords.com row makes the same point against interest: "Feature-rich; the clearest proof that features alone are not the differentiator". That is the opposite of innovation theater.

The one place adjectives sit where bounds belong is §8. "Song pages must be usable on slow mobile connections typical of Sri Lankan mobile data" and "Sheet contrast sufficient for reading in poor light" are the two bullets a UX designer or engineer cannot verify. SM-1 partially rescues the first by supplying "under two seconds" on "a mid-range Android phone over a constrained mobile connection", but the accessibility bullet has no ratio and no standard named anywhere in the document, despite §8 otherwise being unusually product-specific (the "Latin-only web font that delays or breaks script rendering is a defect, not a trade-off" bullet is a model of how to write an NFR).

### Findings

- **medium** §8 accessibility and performance bullets use adjectives where the rest of the section uses bounds — "Sheet contrast sufficient for reading in poor light" names no ratio and no conformance level, and "usable on slow mobile connections" no device or network profile (SM-1 defines these, §8 does not reference it). These are the two §8 bullets that cannot be tested. *Fix:* state a contrast ratio and a named conformance target for the Sheet and Play View, and cross-reference SM-1's device/network profile from the performance bullet.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is stated, and prioritisation follows from it inside each feature area. §4.2 declares Play View "the feature the product intends to be known for" and its `[NOTE FOR PM]` escalates it from requirement to standard: "It should be treated as the quality bar for the whole release, not as one requirement among many." §4.7 declares Ingestion "the release's gating capability and the founder's explicit version-one bet". §4.10 justifies editorial content by decision-independence — "one move that helps regardless of how the two big unknowns resolve" — which is a genuine strategic argument, not a content-marketing reflex.

The Success Metrics are unusually well-aimed. SM-2 is explicitly a thesis metric, not an activity metric: "Share of song page sessions that enter Play View... This is the clearest signal the product is being played rather than read." Four counter-metrics are present and each names what it counterbalances, including SM-C3 against the founder's own catalogue ambition ("Publishing unreviewed Ingestion Candidates would inflate SM-3 while destroying the accuracy the product is differentiating on") and SM-C4 against the growth reflex of gating features behind registration. §12's "Revenue levers, in priority order: pages per session, audience mix, page speed, then catalogue breadth. Ad density is explicitly not a lever" is a prioritisation with teeth.

Two things break the arc. First, §6.1 is a flat unsequenced list — it enumerates all 32 FRs across ten feature areas in FR order, not thesis order, with no phasing. The PRD knows §4.7 is gating and §4.2 is the bet, but §6.1 does not encode that, and it commits community Submissions and Corrections with a multi-language Review Queue, accounts with cross-device migration, user-ordered Collections, a PWA shell, an editorial content surface, and an ingestion pipeline — for an operation §13.4 describes as "Single-operator economics" with infrastructure that "must stay near-zero at low traffic". Second, the business metric is unbounded. SM-4 is a primary metric that states only that it will be "Tracked from first serving" with no target, and no revenue figure appears anywhere in the document, even though §2.1 lists the owner's job as "Generate real advertising income from a catalogue of 5,000 chord sheets that currently earns nothing". SM-3's thresholds are similarly loose — "1,000 published within the first release cycle and a sustained weekly rate thereafter" never defines the cycle length or the rate.

### Findings

- **high** §6.1 MVP scope is unsequenced and unphased — ten feature areas and 32 FRs listed in FR order with no build order, despite the PRD itself ranking §4.7 as "the release's gating capability" and §4.2 as the quality bar for the release. Against §13.4's single-operator economics this reads as a multi-team scope. Epic breakdown will produce ten parallel epics with no dependency order. *Fix:* split §6.1 into an ordered set of releases (for example: sheet rendering + Play View + Transpose on a hand-seeded catalogue, then Ingestion at scale, then Favourites/Collections/accounts, then community contribution), and state which FRs are launch-blocking versus first-cycle.
- **high** SM-4 has no target and the document contains no revenue figure (§7, §2.1) — the owner's own job-to-be-done is income generation, yet no SM states what commercial outcome would count as success, so nobody can judge whether v1 worked on the axis the whole business model rests on. SM-3's "first release cycle" and "sustained weekly rate" are likewise unnumbered. *Fix:* set a first-cycle revenue-per-thousand-pageviews floor and a monthly income figure (tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` if it is a guess, as SM-2's threshold already is), and put a date range and a weekly number on SM-3.
- **low** The thesis's root cause has no v1 answer (§4.3 note, §6.2, OQ-1) — "Any key-detection or key-suggestion capability — deferred pending OQ-1, despite addressing the root cause" is disclosed honestly, but it leaves SM-2 as the sole evidence that the "instrument you play with" thesis is true. The addendum's interval-helper option is described as "deterministic, needs no audio input, and directly addresses the root cause"; nothing in the PRD reflects that it is the frontrunner. *Fix:* either pull the interval helper into scope as a small FR, or state in §6.2 that the thesis is deliberately being tested on Play View alone in v1.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Every FR carries a "Consequences (testable)" block, and in the player-facing features those consequences are the best part of the document. FR-1's "at viewport widths from 320 CSS pixels upward" and "No horizontal scrolling is required to read any lyric line at 320 pixels wide" are bounds, not adjectives. FR-2's "The first section of the Sheet is present in the initial server response and requires no client-side execution to become readable" is a single sentence an engineer can turn into a test. FR-11 is the strongest FR in the document — slash-chord handling, extension preservation, and "Any Chord Event the system cannot parse is displayed unchanged and flagged for review rather than guessed at" close exactly the failure modes that break transpose implementations. FR-27 to FR-29 are similarly unambiguous, down to "No publisher or slot identifier is embedded in application source" and "The advertising provider's script is loaded at most once per page". There is no separate Acceptance section and none is needed; the consequences carry it.

The soft spots cluster in the two features with the most engineering uncertainty, which is the worst place for them. §4.4 Search rests its entire quality bar on unquantified language: FR-14's "Partial and misspelled input still returns the intended Song where a reasonable match exists" is the rubric's flagged phrase almost verbatim, and FR-15's "Common romanised ("Singlish" and "Tanglish") spellings of a title match the native-script Song" leaves "common" to the implementer with no corpus and no test set, while "Artist name variants, nicknames, and shortened forms match the correct artist" names no source of truth for the alias list. Cross-script search for Sinhala and Tamil is one of the two hardest problems in this build, and it has no acceptance bar.

§4.7 Ingestion has the same shape of gap, and it matters more because it gates the release. FR-24 correctly demands that "Content the converter could not interpret is marked as uncertain rather than omitted or invented", but no FR, NFR, or SM states an accuracy threshold at which Ingestion is acceptable. §4.7 defers accuracy to "a technical spike, not to this document" and the addendum says the spike "must produce measured accuracy on a representative sample of the actual 5,000 sheets" — but a spike with a measurement and no threshold has no pass/fail, which means OQ-3 cannot actually close.

### Findings

- **high** Search quality has no acceptance bar (FR-14, FR-15) — "where a reasonable match exists" and "Common romanised... spellings" are the quality definition for the hardest feature in the build. No test corpus, no top-N ranking requirement, no match-rate target, and no named source for artist aliases. *Fix:* commit to a fixed evaluation set (for example 100 real queries in Sinhala script, Tamil script, and romanised form, drawn from the founder's own experience or competitor search logs) with a required top-3 hit rate, and make artist aliases an explicitly curated list.
- **high** Ingestion has no accuracy acceptance threshold (FR-24, FR-25, §4.7, OQ-3) — the gating capability defines behaviour on uncertainty but never states how accurate is accurate enough, so the OQ-3 spike has a measurement obligation and no decision criterion. *Fix:* state the threshold the spike must clear (for example: chord-position accuracy and per-sheet reviewer-correction time, with a maximum acceptable rate of *silent* errors, which the addendum rightly says "matters more than the raw accuracy rate").
- **medium** FR-11 does not state the enharmonic spelling rule — "Resulting chord names are spelled appropriately for the target key rather than always sharp or always flat" is the one soft consequence in an otherwise exemplary FR, and §8 "Trust in determinism" requires that Transpose "be covered by automated tests before release". The test cannot be written from "appropriately". *Fix:* state the rule (spell from the target key's key signature; name the handling for keys with no conventional signature and for modal or ambiguous Sheets).
- **medium** No scale envelope anywhere (§8, §14, §7) — the document never states catalogue size at launch, expected pageviews, or concurrency, though §14 names "Traffic growth degrades performance" as a risk, §8 requires cacheability "so that traffic growth does not degrade response times", and SM-4 is a per-thousand-pageview metric. Architecture has no sizing input. *Fix:* add expected pageviews per month at launch and at the point the revenue metric becomes meaningful, plus published-Song count targets, to §8.
- **medium** FR-26 duplicate detection is unbounded — "appears to duplicate" and "A probable duplicate is surfaced" give no matching basis or threshold, on a feature that will run across 5,000 sheets where many Songs legitimately have multiple Sheets (FR-4). Both false positives and false negatives have real cost. *Fix:* state what is compared (normalised title plus artist plus first lyric line) and whether the warning is advisory or blocking.
- **low** Residual adjective sweep — FR-6 "a single unobtrusive line", FR-28 "leaves no visible gap that breaks the layout", FR-30 "not promoted intrusively", FR-16's trigger "When a search returns nothing useful" (its consequence defines "Zero-result and no-click queries", which are not the same set and a no-click cannot be known at the moment the request form must appear). Individually minor; collectively they are the only untestable phrases left in §4. *Fix:* resolve FR-16's trigger to zero-results explicitly, and let §10's voice guidance carry the rest rather than the FRs.
- **low** Device-held state has no retention or eviction statement (FR-8, FR-13, FR-18, FR-19) — four FRs promise persistence "on that device" and "without an account"; none says what happens when storage is cleared, whether the loss is silent, or whether FR-20 migration has a size limit. SM-5 depends on this state surviving. *Fix:* one line in §4.5 stating that device-held state is best-effort and its loss is silent, or specify the guarantee.

## Scope honesty — strong

§5 does real work. Eight non-goals, each declarative and each with a reason attached rather than a bare exclusion: the tuner and metronome are out because they "were explicitly identified by the founder as removable without losing the weekly habit" *and* because "Browser microphone behaviour is unreliable enough to damage the brand if shipped badly". "This is not a social network... Corrections replace arguments" converts an omission into a design position. The last bullet — "The site does not become an advertising surface with songs attached" — is a non-goal aimed at the founder's own future incentives, which is the hardest kind to write.

§6.2 de-scopes openly rather than silently, and flags its own weak spots: the chord-diagram deferral is tagged `[ASSUMPTION: deferred to v2. The founder never requested them, though every local competitor offers them and beginners stall without them. Flagged as a possible gap.]` and then escalated again as OQ-9. The Learn/Practice/Mastered removal carries a `[NOTE FOR PM]` because it came from the founder's own sketch. The `[ASSUMPTION]` tagging discipline holds across the document and §18 round-trips cleanly.

Open-items density is high — eleven Open Questions, eleven indexed assumptions, five `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts — but for a Fast-path draft heading into founder review that is appropriate rather than a blocker; it is the document doing its job of surfacing what the brainstorm left unresolved. It would be a blocker if this were green-lit to build, and the document's `status: draft` and §0's framing are consistent with that.

The one genuinely silent scope commitment is multi-language moderation. §13.1 states "Moderation must cover every published Language. Sinhala, Tamil, Hindi, and English content all require review by someone who can read it", and FR-23 makes it a testable consequence ("Review is possible on content in Sinhala, Tamil, Hindi, and English"). But §13.4 describes a single operator, and nothing in §17 or §18 asks whether that operator reads Tamil or Hindi, or who would. For an obligation the PRD itself classifies as policy compliance on ad-bearing pages, that is an unflagged dependency on a person who may not exist.

### Findings

- **high** Four-language moderation obligation has no resourcing path and is not flagged as an assumption (§13.1, FR-23, §13.4) — the PRD requires human review "by someone who can read it" for Sinhala, Tamil, Hindi, and English, ties it to advertising-policy compliance, and elsewhere describes a single-operator business, without an `[ASSUMPTION]` or OQ on reviewer language coverage. The practical consequence is that Tamil and Hindi Submissions either block or ship unreviewed. *Fix:* either add an `[ASSUMPTION]` on reviewer coverage plus an OQ on recruiting reviewers, or restrict community contribution to the languages the operator can review in v1 and say so in §5.
- **medium** Hindi is required by constraints but appears in no requirement (§8, §13.1, FR-23 versus §4.4, §6.1) — §8 demands correct Devanagari first paint and §13.1 demands Hindi moderation, but FR-15 covers only Sinhala, Tamil, and romanisation, and §6.1 scopes "Search across Sinhala, Tamil, and English". Hindi carries obligations with no feature support and no non-goal disclaiming it. *Fix:* either add Hindi to FR-15 and §6.1, or move Hindi to §5/§6.2 and strike it from §8 and §13.1.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This PRD is explicitly chain-top: §0 says it exists "for the downstream BMad workflows that will consume it — UX, architecture, and epic breakdown", so this dimension carries full weight. Much of the machinery for that is in place and well done. §3 is a real glossary of eighteen domain nouns with genuine definitional content, not a word list — the Sheet/Song distinction, the Original Key versus Sounding Key pair, and the Discover Surface definition ("the non-sheet portion of a song page") each pre-empt an ambiguity that would otherwise surface in implementation. FR, UJ, and SM IDs are contiguous and unique, "Realises UJ-n" back-references appear on the FRs that earn them, and each SM names the FRs it validates. Feature descriptions in §4 carry their own rationale, so a section pulled out alone still explains why it exists. The addendum does its job too: it keeps mechanism out of the PRD while giving the architect a starting shape, and it correctly derives that shape from stated requirements ("That set of consequences rules out storing chords as display strings").

The blocking problem is that three capabilities the PRD commits to have no FR to source from. §6.1 puts "Published Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, and a working takedown route (§13)" in MVP scope, and §13.1 specifies that route in detail — "a published contact, a request mechanism, a log of requests and outcomes, and the ability to unpublish within minutes" — but only the unpublish half exists as a requirement (FR-23). §13.1 requires that "Lyrics must be structurally removable... This makes a chord-only fallback and a fast takedown response possible", and §14 lists "chord-only fallback prepared" as a mitigation for the top risk row, but no FR describes a Sheet rendering without lyrics. And §8 Observability requires that "Failed searches, Correction volume, Play View entry and completion, Transpose and Capo use, and advertising performance must be measurable" while all eight SMs depend on instrumentation that no FR delivers. These are the mitigations for the risk §13.1 calls "an existential event, not an inconvenience", and epic breakdown has nothing to turn them into.

Below that, three naming issues will cost architecture real time. "Language" is used throughout as a capitalised domain noun ("belongs to exactly one primary Language", "Language Page", "Results indicate the Language of each Song") but has no §3 entry and its permitted values are never enumerated — and §13.1 implies four while §6.1 implies three. "Administrator" and "operator" are used as if they were different roles (FR-29 "An operator can set the publisher identifier" against FR-23 "An administrator can work the Review Queue"), with neither defined and no role model anywhere. And "cache" carries two incompatible meanings: FR-31's device-side cache, whose consequence reads "Song content is fetched from the network, not served from cache when disconnected", against §8's server/CDN cache, which requires that "Song pages must be servable from cache or a content delivery network". Read literally and in isolation, FR-31 forbids what §8 mandates.

### Findings

- **critical** Three MVP-committed capabilities have no FR (§6.1, §13.1, §8, §14) — (a) the takedown request mechanism and its log of requests and outcomes, (b) the chord-only / lyrics-removed Sheet rendering that §14 names as mitigation for the PRD's top risk, and (c) analytics instrumentation for SM-1 to SM-8 and the §8 Observability list. All three are in scope, two are launch-blocking by the PRD's own account, and none can be source-extracted into a story. *Fix:* add a §4.11 (or extend §4.6) with FRs for the takedown intake and log, for rendering a Sheet with lyrics suppressed while Chord Event structure survives, and for the measurement events each SM requires.
- **medium** "Language" is used as a defined domain noun but is undefined and its values are inconsistent (§3, FR-15, FR-17, §6.1, §13.1) — capitalised and load-bearing (a Song "belongs to exactly one primary Language"; Language Pages are indexable per FR-17) with no glossary entry and no enumeration; §6.1 implies three languages, §13.1 four. "Language Page" is likewise capitalised and used as a surface but appears only inside the Discover Surface definition. *Fix:* add `Language` and `Language Page` entries to §3 with the permitted value set named explicitly.
- **medium** Two undefined administrative roles (FR-23, FR-25, FR-26, FR-29, FR-32, §9) — "administrator" and "operator" are used in distinct FR subjects with no glossary entry and no privilege model, leaving architecture to guess whether they are one role or two. §9 adds "Administration" as a surface without clarifying. *Fix:* define one role in §3, or define both and state which FRs each can perform.
- **medium** "Cache" is overloaded across FR-31 and §8 — FR-31's "not served from cache when disconnected" concerns device-side storage, §8's "servable from cache or a content delivery network" concerns edge caching, and nothing distinguishes them. FR-31 read alone contradicts the §8 cacheability NFR that the addendum treats as the core rendering strategy. *Fix:* name the two separately (device cache versus edge cache) in §3 or inline in FR-31.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is a consumer product with meaningful UX and a chain-top position, so UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing — and they are present, named, and consequential, each with entry state, path, climax, resolution, and an edge case that does real work (UJ-1's "he is offered a request form pre-filled with what he typed" becomes FR-16; UJ-2's edge case is where OQ-1 first surfaces). Five UJs for ten feature areas is restraint, not density: there is no UJ-per-FR padding, and §4.7's administrative Ingestion is specified as a capability spec with a single owner-facing journey (UJ-5) rather than being dressed up in visitor-journey clothing. That is the right call for a surface with exactly one user.

Two sections that would be filler in most PRDs are earned here. §16 Competitive Landscape changes the document's conclusions rather than decorating them — its finding that transpose and catalogue size are table stakes is what makes §4.2 the bet. §15 Why Now is three sentences and carries specific evidence. §13.1's opening line — "This section records risk, not legal advice. Qualified counsel is required before the catalogue goes public." — correctly sizes what a PRD can and cannot settle. The choice to push mechanism into `addendum.md` and hold §4 at the behavioural level is consistently executed; there is very little mechanism leakage in the PRD proper.

The only shape tension is between the document's formality and the operation it describes. The rigor level here is appropriate to a funded consumer launch, and the founder is a single operator with near-zero infrastructure budget (§13.4). The formality itself is fine — the stakes are launch-stakes and the document earns its length. What does not fit is the *scope* that formality is applied to, which is covered under Strategic coherence rather than repeated here.
