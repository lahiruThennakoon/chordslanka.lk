# PRD Quality Review — chordslanka.lk (re-review)

- **PRD:** `_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-chordslanka.lk-2026-09-27/prd.md`
- **Addendum:** `addendum.md` (read, assessed)
- **Rubric:** `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`
- **Pass:** second rubric walk, after ~20 edits responding to `review-rubric.md` and `review-adversarial.md`

## Overall verdict

The edits closed almost every gap of *omission*. The PRD now states a revenue figure and its arithmetic (§12.1), resolves its own internal contradiction rather than hiding it (§12.2), specifies discoverability, rights response and measurement as requirements rather than assumptions (§4.11–§4.13), fixes the build order (§6.0), and records the founder's non-quantitative pass/fail bar (§7.0). The ID space came through clean: FR-1..FR-40 each appear exactly once with no gaps or duplicates, SM-1..SM-9 and SM-C1..SM-C4 all resolve, and §6.1 accounts for all forty FRs. That is a better outcome than twenty edits usually produce.

What the edits introduced is integrity damage. FR-38 — the new requirement whose entire job is to make §7 measurable — claims "every metric SM-1 to SM-9 can be computed from recorded data without manual estimation" while its own event list omits the inputs for five of them and SM-9's ranking half is not site-recordable at all. §12.1's arithmetic compares required *pageviews* against competitor *visits* and cites a working model that does not exist in the workspace. And roughly a dozen places carry text that was true before an edit and is not true now — §5 still forbids what FR-39 requires, the Glossary's Review Queue still excludes what FR-23 and FR-36 put in it, four rules now have two owners apiece.

Not a green light. But the remaining work is repair against a settled document rather than discovery, with two exceptions that need real thinking: whether §12.1's economics actually close, and what "best Autoscroll of any chord site" means as a testable release condition.

## 1. Decision-readiness — adequate

This dimension is where the edits did their best work, and it is also where the one remaining genuinely hard problem sits.

The document now states decisions as decisions. §13.1 says "Publish Sheets with full lyrics, and compete on responsiveness rather than avoidance" and then spends six bullets on what that obliges, ordered by importance, with the load-bearing one named ("Remove first, discuss afterwards"). §13.2 says the name proximity is "a deliberate strategy, not an accepted accident" and then names the residual risk as "a total one." §6.0 locks a sequencing decision and gives the practical reason rather than a principled one. OQ-3 sets abort thresholds *in advance* — "so the result cannot be rationalised" — which is the single most decision-ready paragraph in the document. §12.2 is a model of the genre: it names a tension ("Pages per session is a top revenue lever, yet FR-5 and FR-27 make Play View ad-free"), states which side wins, and says what is out of bounds as a result. None of this is smoothed to neutral.

What stops this being *strong* is that the number every one of those decisions is now meant to be judged against does not verify. §12.1 exists precisely because "this document previously specified no figure — which allowed a plan to look sound while the arithmetic underneath it did not close." The new arithmetic has the same problem one level down: it needs "roughly 100,000 to 300,000 ad-bearing pageviews per month" and then compares that to a field measured in *visits* ("the market leader draws about 151,000 visits per month and the whole visible field totals roughly 337,000") to conclude "this means taking a leading share of the market — not a slice of it." Those are different units, and §16 records the leader's "23:05 average session," which implies several pageviews per visit. The conclusion may be too pessimistic; it may be too optimistic once the ad-free Play View share is netted out. It cannot be checked, because the cited model is absent.

The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions (§4.3's root-cause argument, §4.10's cost-versus-policy bind, §4.9's offline carve-out, §4.2's comparative bar) rather than at safe checkpoints. Open Questions are genuinely open where they are open: OQ-3, OQ-7, OQ-8, OQ-10 and the residual half of OQ-11 all lack answers, and none of them is rhetorical.

### Findings

- **critical** §12.1's arithmetic mixes units and its supporting model is missing (§12.1) — The target requires "100,000 to 300,000 ad-bearing pageviews per month" and is then judged against competitor *visits* (~151,000 leader, ~337,000 field) to conclude the plan "means taking a leading share of the market." Pageviews and visits are not comparable, and §16's own "23:05 average session" suggests the multiple is well above one. Compounding it: "The full working model, including the measured competitive traffic data and break-even arithmetic, is at `chordslanka-business-case.canvas.tsx` in this workspace's canvases folder" — there is no such file and no canvases folder anywhere in the repository. The section whose stated purpose is to stop the plan "look[ing] sound while the arithmetic underneath it did not close" therefore cannot be audited, and §14's "Market is smaller than the income target needs" row pivots on it. *Fix:* restate the requirement in pageviews and the market in pageviews, using an explicit pageviews-per-visit assumption tagged `[ASSUMPTION]`; net out the ad-free share implied by SM-2's 25% Play View target; then either commit the working model to this folder or delete the reference and inline the three lines of arithmetic.
- **medium** §7.0's qualitative gate has no judge and no procedure (§7.0) — "If the experience is poor, the product has failed regardless of the numbers" and "if UX is not good it is still a failure" are the right instinct, and the fourth bullet's end-to-end walk is close to a real gate. But nothing says who performs the walk, on which device, against what pass condition, or who is empowered to fail the release. As written it is a sentiment that cannot block anything. *Fix:* name the artefact — a single scripted walkthrough (search → open → transpose → play → scroll) on a named mid-range Android device, performed by the founder, with an explicit "would I hand this to a friend at a gathering" verdict recorded before release.
- **low** §13.1's appeal to market practice overstates what §16 records (§13.1) — "This matches every site in §16, all of which publish full lyrics under advertising" — §16's table does not record lyric or advertising behaviour per site, and lklyrics.com is characterised as "Lyrics-led rather than chords-led," a different posture. *Fix:* either add the observation to the §16 table or soften to the sites that were actually checked.

## 2. Substance over theater — strong

Very little furniture. Five UJs, every one with a named protagonist carrying age, skill level and physical context inline, and every one cited by at least one FR ("Realises UJ-1", etc.) — UJ-1 by FR-1/FR-2/FR-14/FR-16, UJ-2 by FR-11/FR-13, UJ-3 by FR-13/FR-18/FR-19, UJ-4 by FR-21, UJ-5 by FR-24/FR-25. No sixth persona added to look thorough. UJ-2's edge case ("If she cannot tell which way to move, she guesses and corrects") does real work: it is the argument §4.3 then builds the whole transpose quality bar on.

The NFRs are product-specific rather than boilerplate: "viewport widths from 320 CSS pixels upward," "under two seconds," "a click-through rate above roughly 2% is treated as an invalid-activity signal," "USD 1–3" RPM, "Chord identity is never conveyed by colour alone." §8's momentum bullet is the opposite of NFR theater — it quotes the founder, names the four FRs that serve it, and states the correct failure behaviour ("degrade quietly and keep the Sheet readable rather than to explain, block, or reload").

The differentiation claim is earned rather than asserted. §16 does not just list competitors; it extracts two findings and says why they matter ("The leader's 23-minute average session is independent evidence for this product's central thesis") and it records a finding that cuts *against* the plan (visitors leaving for chordify.net and smule.com — "evidence that the local offering does not finish the job"). A PRD that records inconvenient competitive evidence is not performing thoroughness.

§7.0 is the standout new addition. "Traffic in month one is not success. Arrivals prove the site was found, not that it worked" is exactly the anti-theater statement a metrics section usually lacks.

### Findings

- **high** §14's audience-mix mitigation cites a capability no requirement specifies (§14, §12.1, §4.10) — The risk row "Local-only audience caps revenue" is mitigated by "Editorial content in English; platform-level multi-language support from day one," and §12.1 states "English-language editorial content and diaspora-oriented material are the only levers that touch" the largest multiplier in the revenue model. FR-32 says nothing about language, audience, or diaspora; its consequences are about placement, coverage and indexing only. The single largest revenue lever in the document has no requirement behind it. *Fix:* either add a consequence to FR-32 fixing the language and audience target of editorial coverage, or drop the claim from §12.1 and §14 and move audience mix wholly into OQ-7 as an unaddressed lever.
- **low** §1 overstates §16's own evidence twice (§1) — "the largest incumbent has songs with over 250,000 views each" generalises §16's single data point ("top Song at 262k views"). "Half a dozen Sinhala chord sites already exist" undercounts §16's ten rows; §12.2 says "six alternatives." *Fix:* "its most-viewed song has passed 250,000 views" and "roughly ten."

## 3. Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is clear, stated early, and argued rather than asserted: every competitor is "a catalogue you read. This one is an instrument you play with," the gap is "not more songs, but a live catalogue and a sheet that behaves well while a guitar is in the way," and §16 closes with the same conclusion reached from evidence ("catalogue size and transpose are table stakes in this market, not differentiators"). The MVP scope kind is coherent — revenue-shaped, with §12.1 and §12.2 making the scope logic explicit. §6.0's build order follows from the thesis rather than from what is easy. Counter-metrics exist and are well chosen; SM-C3 in particular ("Publishing unreviewed Ingestion Candidates would inflate SM-3 while destroying the accuracy the product is differentiating on") is a counter-metric that understands what it is protecting.

The incoherence is in the seam between the thesis and the metrics. The thesis says the playing experience is the differentiator. §4.2 sets the bar as a comparative one — Autoscroll "must be **the best of any chord site or app he has used**, not merely present" — and its note says it "should be treated as the quality bar for the whole release, not as one requirement among many." Nothing in §7 tests that. SM-2 measures Play View *entry rate*, which is a proxy for intent, not for quality; a visitor can enter Play View and find the scroll unusable and SM-2 rises. §7.0 was added to carry exactly this kind of non-quantitative bar and does not mention Autoscroll. The one thing the product intends to be famous for has no comparison set, no evaluator, and no pass condition.

The same seam shows in Transpose. §4.3 is the most strategically load-bearing section in the document — it names the root cause, rejects three candidate solutions, and lands on "cheap iteration, not detection." Its own assumption then says "SM-2 and Transpose usage should be read together after launch to check that visitors adjust keys rather than abandoning songs." There is no Transpose usage metric. FR-10, FR-11, FR-12, FR-13 and FR-40 — the entire differentiating cluster — are validated by no SM. FR-38 lists "Transpose and Capo use" as instrumented, so the data will exist; nothing in §7 reads it.

Finally, §12's lever ordering and §12.1's lever claim disagree, and the disagreement is consequential because it is the stated reason a narrowed FR-32 survived.

### Findings

- **critical** The release's signature capability has no bar and no metric (§4.2, §7, §7.0) — Autoscroll must be "the best of any chord site or app he has used" and is to be "the quality bar for the whole release." No comparison set is named, no evaluation method is given, no SM covers FR-7, and §7.0 — added specifically to hold non-quantitative bars — omits it. "As good as the competition" is declared an explicit failure with no way to establish whether it has occurred. For a PRD whose whole thesis is that the playing experience is the differentiator, this is the gap that matters most. *Fix:* name the three or four apps the comparison is against (slchords.com's full-screen view plus two named mobile apps), list the specific behaviours the bar consists of — smoothness at low speeds, no stutter on speed change, manual nudge without losing the scroll (already in FR-7), thumb reachability — and add a §7.0 bullet making a side-by-side comparison on a real phone a release condition with a named judge.
- **high** No metric validates Transpose, Capo, or Chord Diagrams, and §4.3 cites a metric that does not exist (§4.3, §7) — §4.3's assumption instructs the reader to read "SM-2 and Transpose usage" together after launch; no such metric is defined. FR-10 to FR-13 and FR-40 appear in no SM's *Validates* list, despite being the cluster §16 calls table stakes and §4.3 calls the answer to the root cause. FR-38 already requires the events. *Fix:* add an SM for transpose engagement (share of Play View sessions that transpose; share that transpose more than twice, as the guess-and-correct signal) and cite FR-11/FR-12/FR-13; add diagram-open rate under it or explicitly record that FR-40 is unmeasured in v1.
- **high** §12.1 contradicts §12 on lever priority (§12, §12.1) — §12 lists "Revenue levers, in priority order: traffic volume from organic search, pages per session, audience mix, page speed, then catalogue breadth" — audience mix third. §12.1 states "Audience mix is the largest single multiplier in the model." Both cannot be true, and the §12.1 version is what justifies keeping FR-32 ("which is why FR-32 survives despite being narrowed"). *Fix:* reorder §12's list to match §12.1 and say what changed, or qualify §12.1 to "the largest multiplier available after traffic volume."
- **medium** Two gameable metrics have no counter-metric, one of them flagged by the PRD itself (§7, §12.2) — §12.2 states "Length extracted by obstruction is explicitly out of bounds," which is precisely the way SM-8 (pages per session) gets gamed, yet SM-C1 to SM-C4 counterbalance only SM-3, SM-4 and SM-5. SM-2 is similarly uncovered: driving Play View entry reduces ad-bearing pageviews, which §12.2 accepts as the intended trade but does not bound. *Fix:* add a counter-metric for SM-8 (e.g. pages per session must not rise while time-to-song rises, or clicks-to-reach-a-Sheet must not increase release over release), and state the floor below which SM-2's cannibalisation of SM-8 would prompt review.

## 4. Done-ness clarity — adequate

Most FRs would survive contact with an engineer, and several are unusually good. FR-1's "viewport widths from 320 CSS pixels upward" and "No horizontal scrolling is required to read any lyric line at 320 pixels wide" are directly testable. FR-2 specifies the mechanism of the guarantee rather than an adjective ("present in the initial server response and requires no client-side execution to become readable"). FR-7's "A visitor can scroll or nudge the Sheet by hand *while Autoscroll is running*, and Autoscroll continues from the new position rather than stopping or snapping back" is the kind of consequence that only comes from someone imagining the real situation. FR-40 goes further and states the defect rather than the behaviour — "A diagram that shows the sounding chord while a capo is on is a defect, not an approximation" — which removes the ambiguity a positive statement would leave. FR-24's "Content the converter could not interpret is marked as uncertain rather than omitted or invented" is testable and load-bearing.

The failures cluster in three places: the new FR-38, which over-claims; the new FR-34, which is unfalsifiable at its most important point; and FR-11's respelling rule, which is the one place in the document where an undefined adjective sits on the trust-critical path.

FR-11 deserves the detail. Its consequences are otherwise excellent — slash chords, preserved extensions, untouched annotations, unparseable events flagged rather than guessed, scroll position preserved, no flicker on rapid steps. Then: "Resulting chord names are spelled appropriately for the target key rather than always sharp or always flat." *Appropriately* is doing all the work, and §8 requires that "Transpose and Capo are pure, testable transformations ... covered by automated tests before release." An engineer cannot write that test. Is F♯ or G♭ correct in the key of D♭ major? Does a transposed sheet in B major stay in sharps when the source was flats? Does the Sounding Key drive spelling, or the Capo-relative shape? §14 rates a wrong transposition as "Permanent loss of trust in the site's core competence," and the addendum's chord-representation section assumes the rule exists ("respell toward the target key") without stating it either.

### Findings

- **critical** FR-38 contradicts itself and leaves SM-9 uninstrumentable (§4.13) — FR-38 asserts "Every metric SM-1 to SM-9 can be computed from recorded data without manual estimation," but its enumerated events — "Time to first readable Sheet, Play View entry and exit, Autoscroll start and speed changes, Transpose and Capo use, search queries including zero-result queries, Favourite and Collection actions, and advertising performance" — do not include returning-visitor identification (SM-5), pageview or session counting (SM-2's denominator, SM-8 entirely), traffic source (SM-9), Correction resolution time (SM-6), or Song Request conversion and subsequent publication (SM-7). SM-9 also requires "the count of Songs ranking on the first page for their own title" and the brand-term position, neither of which is recordable on the site at all — they come from an external search console. §4.13's own premise is "An unmeasured metric is a wish"; as written, five of nine remain wishes and the leading indicator for the whole revenue plan is uninstrumented. *Fix:* extend the event list to cover session, pageview, referrer and visitor-recurrence, Correction lifecycle timestamps, and Song Request outcomes; then split the ranking half of SM-9 into a separate consequence naming the external data source and stating that it is reported rather than computed.
- **high** FR-34's key consequence is unfalsifiable and its second is unbuildable (§4.11) — "Every published Song is reachable by following links from the homepage within a small, bounded number of hops" has no number, and it is the only crawl-reachability guarantee for a 5,000-song catalogue behind a homepage §9 calls "Deliberately light: most visitors never see it." Separately, the FR requires links to Songs sharing "same Collection theme," but Glossary defines a Collection as "A user-named, user-ordered set of Songs" and §13.3 requires Collections to "live on the device until an account exists" — the system cannot derive public themes from them. The phrase also sits uneasily with §5's non-goal that "The product is never framed around occasions." *Fix:* state the hop count (three is the conventional target) and the surfaces that deliver it (Artist Page, Language Page, recently-published surface, sitemap); replace "same Collection theme" with a named editorial or metadata relation, or delete that branch.
- **high** FR-11's respelling rule is undefined on the trust-critical path (§4.3, §8, §14) — "spelled appropriately for the target key" is the only consequence in FR-11 that cannot be tested as written, and §8 requires automated coverage of exactly this before release while §14 rates a wrong transposition as permanent loss of trust. The addendum assumes the rule rather than supplying it. *Fix:* state the rule — e.g. spelling follows the key signature of the Sounding Key, with a named enharmonic preference for each of the twelve keys and a stated behaviour for keys with no conventional signature — or record it as an open question with an owner. It is a table, not a paragraph.
- **medium** SM-1's threshold has no reproducible harness (§7, §4.1) — "on a mid-range Android phone over a constrained mobile connection, under two seconds" names neither a device class nor a bandwidth and latency profile, so two measurements can disagree without either being wrong. FR-2 shares the vagueness ("a constrained mobile connection"). The threshold is otherwise the document's sharpest number. *Fix:* name the test profile once (device tier, throttled throughput, RTT) in §7 and have FR-2 refer to it.
- **medium** FR-14 and FR-15 need a fixture set to be testable (§4.4) — "Partial and misspelled input still returns the intended Song where a reasonable match exists" and "Common romanised ('Singlish' and 'Tanglish') spellings of a title match the native-script Song" are the right capabilities expressed in a way no test can assert. FR-15 is also one of the harder pieces of work in the release (the addendum flags "Sinhala Unicode normalisation and Tamil normalisation are separate pieces of work"). *Fix:* add a consequence requiring a named, versioned set of query/expected-Song pairs — twenty or so spanning both scripts, romanisation variants and artist nicknames — as the acceptance artefact.
- **medium** FR-39 introduces sync semantics with no conflict rules (§4.9) — "Favourite and Collection changes made while disconnected are retained on the device and reconciled when the network returns" is the only reconciliation requirement in the document. FR-20 covers one-time migration on registration, not ongoing merge, and nothing says what happens when the same Collection is reordered on two devices or a Song is removed on one and reordered on the other. *Fix:* state the conflict rule (last-write-wins per Collection, or union-with-local-order) or scope FR-39's disconnected writes to Favourites only.
- **medium** SM-6, SM-7, SM-8 and SM-9 carry no thresholds (§7) — §7.0 opens "Every metric below is quantitative," and four of nine have no target: SM-6 is "Volume ... and median days to resolution," SM-8 is "Pages per session" with a direction only, SM-9 is a count with no figure. SM-5's "rising release over release" is directional. Four of nine are therefore dashboards, not gates. *Fix:* set a first-release figure for each, tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` as SM-2's already is, or move them under a "tracked, not gated" heading.
- **medium** "Primary" mixes release gates with multi-year trackers (§7, §12.1) — SM-1, SM-2 and SM-3 carry release thresholds. SM-4 measures "Absolute monthly revenue against the USD 300 target" which §12.1 explicitly frames as "a two-to-three-year effort, not a launch outcome," and SM-9 is similarly long-horizon. Read as a release gate list, Primary is unreachable by the document's own timescale. *Fix:* label each Primary metric as a release gate or a trajectory metric.
- **low** Three different unpublish speeds coexist without being reconciled (FR-36, §8, §13.1) — FR-36 says "immediately," §8 and §13.1 say "within minutes," §13.1 also commits to "unpublish within one business day of a credible request." These are consistent if read as capability versus operational commitment, but the document never says so. *Fix:* one sentence in §13.1 distinguishing the two.
- **low** SM-1 cites an FR that does not apply to its scenario (§7) — SM-1 measures arrival on a song page and "Validates FR-1, FR-2, FR-31," but FR-31 is repeat-visit shell caching and UJ-1's entry state is a first arrival from Google. *Fix:* drop FR-31 from SM-1 or add a repeat-visit measurement.

## 5. Scope honesty — strong

§5 is the best section in the document. Nine non-goals, each stated as a refusal with its reason, several of them expensive refusals honestly taken — "This is not a paid product ... Correctness and readability are never sold," "The site does not become an advertising surface with songs attached. Ad density is capped by the experience, not by inventory appetite." The last bullet's quoted founder instruction ("whether someone is playing on a Friday or a Sunday, a beginner or a professional, *it should not matter*") is a constraint that will actually stop a future feature.

§6.2 does something rarer: it distinguishes *cut* from *deferred*, item by item, and says which is which. "Learn, Practice, and Mastered progression — **cut, confirmed by the founder during finalize** ... Not a deferral awaiting revisit." "Any key-detection or key-suggestion capability — cut, not deferred." The lyrics-only bullet goes further and names the cost of the refusal: "Real traffic is being left on the table deliberately."

OQ-3 is the model for how to carry an unresolved gating unknown: a named sample size ("at least 50 sheets spanning the best and worst legibility"), three named measurements, thresholds fixed in advance with the reason for fixing them in advance, and a specified fallback ("publish a few hundred of the best sheets by hand, lean on community Submissions for growth, and reset SM-3"). Fifteen assumptions are tagged and indexed; none of them is decorative, and several are the uncomfortable kind (§13.1's full-lyric risk, §13.2's domain risk, §12.1's two-to-three-year timescale).

Open-items density is defensible for a green-light-to-build document: five genuinely open OQs, one partially resolved, and the only *gating* one carries abort thresholds. The honesty failures here are all stale text rather than concealment.

### Findings

- **high** §5 and §2.2 still forbid what FR-39 requires (§5, §2.2, §4.9) — §5's first non-goal reads "**This is not an offline app.** No Sheet downloads, no offline reading, no cached song library," and §2.2 lists "**Offline users.** Deliberately unserved — see §5." FR-39 requires that a Sheet being read "remains fully readable and fully usable if the network drops," with Transpose, Capo, text sizing, section jumps and Autoscroll all continuing to work. That is offline reading of that Sheet. §4.9 carves the exception carefully ("There is one deliberate exception, and it is not a compromise of the position") and OQ-5 explains the reasoning, but neither §5 nor §2.2 was updated, and §5 is the section a downstream epic will treat as the authoritative scope fence. *Fix:* amend §5's bullet to "No Sheet downloads, no library of offline Sheets, no reading a Song not already opened — see FR-39 for the one exception," and point §2.2's bullet at §4.9 as well as §5.
- **high** OQ-3 is gating but §6.1 commits to Ingestion unconditionally (§6.1, §6.2, OQ-3, §13.2) — OQ-3 says "Committing to 5,000 sheets before these numbers exist is the single largest way this release fails" and specifies a fallback plan. §6.1 lists "Catalogue Ingestion with human approval against the Source Artefact (FR-24 to FR-26)" with no condition attached, §13.2 makes catalogue depth a launch condition ("a direct argument for depth of catalogue at launch over an early release"), and §12.1's revenue model assumes catalogue scale. The fallback branch exists in OQ-3 and nowhere in the scope sections. *Fix:* mark the Ingestion line in §6.1 as conditional on OQ-3 clearing its thresholds, and add the manual-publication fallback to §6.1 as the alternative branch rather than leaving it inside an Open Question.
- **medium** FR-20's account-free guarantee is stale after the new sections (§4.5) — "No feature in §4.1 through §4.4 requires an account" was exhaustive before §4.11, §4.12 and §4.13 were added. Those sections each independently restate the rule (FR-35 "Sharing requires no account", FR-36 "reachable from every page without an account"), and SM-C4 states the real intent: "Every visitor-facing capability except cross-device sync works unauthenticated by design." *Fix:* replace FR-20's consequence with SM-C4's formulation so the guarantee cannot go stale again.
- **medium** §6.1 commits to two documents with no requirement behind them (§6.1, §13, FR-22) — "Published Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, and a working takedown route (§13)" — the takedown route is FR-36, but the Terms of Use and Privacy Policy have no FR, and FR-22 depends on the terms existing ("The contributor must affirm the submission terms"). §13.1 calls the rights policy page "a launch blocker." Three launch-blocking artefacts sit in a scope bullet rather than in a requirement. *Fix:* add an FR for the published policy pages with consequences covering the contribution terms FR-22 references, the named contact §13.1 requires, and reachability from every page.
- **medium** §18 index and inline tags do not round-trip (§18, §4.9, §6.2) — §18 carries "**§4.9** — Keeping an already-opened Sheet alive through signal loss (FR-39) is consistent with rejecting offline ... Author's reading of the founder's momentum principle; not separately confirmed," but §4.9 has only a `[NOTE FOR PM]` and no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag — so the load-bearing, unconfirmed premise of a new FR is invisible to anyone reading §4.9. In the other direction, §6.2's Hindi/Tamil bullet ends with a bare `[ASSUMPTION]` carrying no text at all; only §18 says what is assumed. *Fix:* add the inline tag to §4.9's note and give §6.2's marker its content.

## 6. Downstream usability — thin

This is where twenty edits did their damage, and it matters here more than it would elsewhere: §0 declares this document chain-top ("for the downstream BMad workflows that will consume it — UX, architecture, and epic breakdown") and commits to two specific contracts — "Vocabulary is fixed in §3 and used verbatim everywhere else" and global FR numbering "so epics can reference them even if features get reorganised."

The numbering contract held, and held well. All forty FR headings are present, FR-1 through FR-40, each exactly once, with no gaps and no duplicates — verified heading by heading. SM-1 to SM-9 and SM-C1 to SM-C4 each resolve to exactly one definition. UJ-1 to UJ-5 and OQ-1 to OQ-13 are contiguous. Every range expression in §6.1 resolves to real FRs, and §6.1 accounts for all forty — nothing in §4 is missing from scope and nothing in scope is invented. Every `§n` reference I followed resolves to the section the citing text describes, with one exception noted below. That is a better result than the edit volume predicts.

What broke is the vocabulary contract and rule ownership. The Glossary's Review Queue entry — "The administrative backlog of Submissions, Corrections, and Ingestion Candidates awaiting a decision" — now disagrees with FR-23, which says it "distinguishes Submissions, Corrections, Song Requests, and Ingestion Candidates," and FR-36 adds a fourth item type with its own log and no stated relationship to the queue. An architect designing the admin surface has three sources and no way to pick. Separately, four rules now have two owners each, and in the §8-versus-FR-38 case the two owners already state different things.

Glossary drift is confined to newly-added text, which is diagnostic of the edits rather than of the original: **Canonical Song URL** is defined in §3 and used verbatim in §9, but FR-33 and FR-35 say "canonical URL" and "the Song's canonical URL"; **Removal Request** is defined in §3 but appears as "content removal request" in FR-36's title and "removal request" in §13.1 and §4.12.

Each section does stand alone reasonably well, and cross-references are mostly by section rather than "see above" — FR-40 citing FR-12 and FR-11 for the capo and unparseable-chord rules is exactly the right pattern.

### Findings

- **high** The Review Queue is defined three incompatible ways (§3, FR-23, FR-36) — Glossary: "Submissions, Corrections, and Ingestion Candidates."
