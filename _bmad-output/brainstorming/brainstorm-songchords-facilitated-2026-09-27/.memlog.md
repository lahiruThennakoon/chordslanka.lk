- (technique) started Role Playing
- (idea) The beginner should not care which day it is. Friday or Sunday, the experience is the same. No day-based or event-based framing.
- (idea) The experienced guitarist should be able to suggest a correction rather than complain. Design the suggestion path, not a complaint channel.
- (idea) Offline should NOT be supported. Ad revenue is the main goal, and offline use earns nothing. This reverses the earlier discovery assumption that offline packs were a premium direction.
- (technique) started First Principles Thinking
- (idea) A song must default to its original scale or key. The original is the starting truth, not a user preference.
- (idea) The user must see the full chord sheet at once, in readable fonts. Readability is a requirement, not a setting.
- (idea) The user must be able to transpose from that original key.
- (technique) started Reverse Brainstorming
- (idea) Traffic in month one is not success. If the UX is poor the product has still failed, no matter the visitor count.
- (technique) started Five Whys
- (idea) People screenshot chords because searching again every time is not easy. The screenshot is a shortcut past repeat search.
- (idea) Give users a favorites list they can add to and remove from, so they never re-search a song they already found.
- (idea) Let users build their own collections by occasion and theme: wedding songs, trip songs, love songs, songs about mother and father.
- (idea) Typing a song name, or any delay at all, kills the enthusiasm of a trip or party singalong. The moment is social and it does not wait.
- (idea) During the pause someone in the group just starts singing, in whatever pitch they landed on, and then the guitarist is stuck chasing that key by ear.
- (insight) Root cause forming: the singer sets the key, not the guitarist, so the product must be able to catch up to a key already in the air.
- (idea) Only a pro can find the key by ear quickly. Everyone else cannot, so the ability to follow a singer is currently a skill gate, not a tool.
- (idea) The missing thing is years of experience. Following a singer by ear is earned, not taught in the moment.
- (insight) Five Whys root cause: the screenshot habit traces down to a skill gap. Searching is slow, the pause lets a singer set the key, and only an experienced ear can catch that key fast. The product can substitute for that experience.
- (technique) started Provocation Technique
- (idea) With an empty library the product still stands on tools: a built-in AI song analyser, a guitar tuner, and request-a-song.
- (insight) Principle hiding in the provocation: the catalog is not the whole product. Tools work on any song, including one that was never in the database.
- (idea) Request-a-song turns a missing song into a demand signal instead of a dead end.
- (technique) started Assumption Reversal
- (idea) Reversal: the chord sheet is not necessarily the centre. The tools around it, guitar tuner and AI chord analyser, carry their own usability weight and can pull users in on their own.
- (technique) started Question Storming
- (question) Biggest open question: how do we get songs into the database when the existing chord sheets we have are JPEG images and PDFs?
- (insight) This is the real cold-start problem for this founder. The source material is not text, so ingestion is an image-to-structured-chords problem.
- (direction) Content is NOT a risk: the founder already owns a song bank of around 5000 chord sheets, stored in Google Drive as JPEG and PDF.
- (question) The real unknown is the pipeline: how to read those 5000 images and PDFs and turn them into structured text the site can use.
- (insight) This changes the cold-start story completely. The earlier discovery assumed a 30-song manual transcription sprint. The actual asset is 5000 sheets already in hand, gated behind conversion.
- (idea) Loop: more guitarists means more AdSense income, but site performance degrades under the load. Revenue and speed pull against each other.
- (insight) Leverage point implied: cheap-to-serve pages. If a song page is static and cacheable, growth raises income without the performance penalty.

- (technique) started TRIZ Contradiction

- (technique) started Fishbone Diagram
- (idea) Wrong chords come from the source that was extracted. The error is inherited from whoever made the original sheet, not introduced by us.
- (insight) Consequence: the 5000-sheet bank carries unknown accuracy. Conversion copies both the chords and the mistakes, which is why the correction path from experienced players matters.
- (technique) started Build on What Works
- (idea) What already works: there are many Sinhala chord sites already, and the founder believes their main income is AdSense. The model is proven locally; the founder wants to out-earn them.
- (question) User asked directly for research on how to earn more profit from AdSense on this kind of site.
- (insight) Research: music is an entry-level AdSense niche, roughly 4 to 10 USD RPM worldwide, driven by about 1.2 percent CTR and 0.35 USD CPC. Country mix matters more than niche.
- (insight) Research: Sri Lankan-audience sites are reported around 1 to 3 USD RPM, while the same content aimed at US, UK, Canada and Australia readers is reported at 5 to 25 USD. Local-only traffic caps earnings hard.
- (insight) Research: Google Publisher Policies ban content that infringes copyright, and publishers are responsible for user-generated content on ad-bearing pages. Reproducing full lyrics without a reprint licence is the stated approval and suspension risk.
- (insight) Research: AdSense guidance for music sites warns against copied lyrics, thin pages, ads beside players, and CTR above about 2 percent, which can look like accidental clicks.
- (insight) Research: Mediavine Journey now accepts sites from about 1000 monthly sessions, and Ezoic requires about 250000 monthly users for new publishers. A higher-paying network later is a real lever, so AdSense need not be permanent.
- (insight) Implication for profit: the levers are audience country mix, pages per session, original written content around the songs, and page speed, not ad density.
- (technique) started Scenario Cross
- (insight) SYNTHESIS 1 - the missing feature the founder described without naming: the Five Whys root cause was experience, because only a pro can catch a key a singer has already started in. Original-key default plus transpose plus that root cause add up to a key-catching helper that tells the player which way to move. Nothing in the feature list does this yet, and it is the one place the product can replace years of ear training.
- (insight) SYNTHESIS 2 - the cut list is more truthful than the fallback. The AI analyser and tuner are what the founder would reach for if the catalog vanished, but the founder also named them as removable without losing the weekly habit. Catalog, search, favourites, transpose and autoscroll are load-bearing; the tools are not.
- (insight) SYNTHESIS 3 - favourites and collections serve the returning user, but the emotionally urgent moment is the gathering, entered from a Google search. Song-page ranking and first-load speed serve that moment; favourites do not. Both worth building, only one urgent.
- (insight) SYNTHESIS 4 - the core tension: local Sinhala and Tamil repertoire is the differentiator but reportedly earns 1 to 3 USD RPM against 5 to 25 for Western traffic, and full lyrics are the exact content Google publisher policy names, with the publisher liable for user submissions too. The differentiator is also the lowest-paying and highest-risk content.
- (insight) SYNTHESIS 5 - the move that survives all four scenario-cross futures is original written content around each song. It makes pages defensible if AdSense refuses, lifts pages per session if traffic stays local, ranks in English if traffic goes international, and creates ad inventory if approved.
